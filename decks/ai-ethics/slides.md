# AI Ethics & Responsible Use

Using AI well means knowing its limits — and its risks

---
transition: slide-up
---

# Why This Matters for Business

<v-clicks>

- AI makes mistakes — and those mistakes can be expensive or embarrassing
- Data you share with AI tools may not be private
- AI outputs can introduce legal, reputational, or regulatory risk
- Teams that use AI without guardrails create liability
- Responsible use builds trust — with customers, regulators, and employees

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

Understanding what can go wrong is the most practical AI skill of all.

</v-click>

---
transition: slide-up
---

# Hallucinations — AI Confidently Gets It Wrong

<v-clicks>

- AI models generate text based on patterns — they **don't look things up**
- When they don't know something, they often **make it up convincingly**
- Fake statistics, wrong citations, non-existent laws, incorrect product specs
- The output looks just as confident whether it's accurate or not

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

**Rule:** Never publish or act on AI-generated facts without verifying them from an authoritative source.

</v-click>

---
transition: slide-up
---

# Common Hallucination Traps

<v-clicks>

- **Legal and regulatory claims** — AI may cite laws that don't exist
- **Statistics and research** — may fabricate plausible-sounding numbers
- **People and quotes** — may attribute false statements to real people
- **Technical specifications** — may describe features that don't exist
- **Recent events** — AI has a knowledge cutoff and may be outdated

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

**How to reduce it:** Ask AI to cite sources, or use tools like Perplexity that link to real sources.

</v-click>

transition: slide-up

# Data Privacy — What You Share Matters

<v-clicks>

- By default, some AI tools may use your conversations to train their models
- Confidential business data, customer PII, and financial information should **never** be pasted into public AI tools
- Check each tool's privacy settings and enterprise data agreements
- Many tools offer **private/enterprise modes** where data is not used for training

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

**Rule:** Treat AI chat windows like email. Don't put anything in them you wouldn't want forwarded.

</v-click>

---
transition: slide-up
---

# What NOT to Share with Public AI Tools

<v-clicks>

- Customer names, emails, or personal data (GDPR risk)
- Unpublished financial data or forecasts
- Legal documents or privileged communications
- Passwords, API keys, or credentials
- Unreleased product information
- Employee personal information

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

**Safe alternative:** Use anonymised or synthetic data when testing AI workflows with sensitive information.

</v-click>

# Bias in AI Systems

<v-clicks>

- AI models learn from human-generated data — and **inherit human biases**
- Outputs can reflect stereotypes about gender, race, age, profession
- Hiring tools using AI may inadvertently discriminate
- Marketing targeting may exclude or exploit vulnerable groups
- Translation tools may carry cultural assumptions

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

**Rule:** Review AI outputs that affect people — hiring, lending, customer service — with extra scrutiny.

</v-click>

# Copyright and Intellectual Property

<v-clicks>

- AI-generated content exists in a legal grey area in most jurisdictions
- AI is trained on internet content — some of which is copyrighted
- **Text:** generally safe to use, but verify for high-stakes publishing
- **Images:** use commercially-licensed models (Adobe Firefly, Canva) for ad use
- **Code:** GitHub Copilot can suggest code from open-source repos — check licences
- **Music and video:** evolving legislation — tread carefully

</v-clicks>

# When NOT to Use AI

<v-clicks>

- **Final legal or medical advice** — AI is not a substitute for a qualified professional
- **Crisis communications** — tone-sensitive situations need human judgement
- **Performance reviews** — decisions about people need human accountability
- **Real-time safety-critical decisions** — AI errors in these contexts can cause harm
- **Anything requiring emotional intelligence** — AI lacks genuine empathy

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

AI is a **tool**, not a decision-maker. Humans remain responsible for the outcomes.

</v-click>

# Building Responsible AI Habits

<v-clicks>

- **Verify** AI outputs before publishing or acting on them
- **Disclose** AI use where relevant — customers and regulators increasingly expect it
- **Review** AI outputs that affect people for bias and fairness
- **Protect** sensitive data — use enterprise/private modes or anonymise first
- **Stay current** — AI regulation is evolving fast (EU AI Act, GDPR enforcement)

</v-clicks>

# Summary

<v-clicks>

AI **hallucinations** are real — always verify facts from authoritative sources

**Never share** confidential data, PII, or credentials with public AI tools

AI outputs can reflect **bias** — review anything affecting people

**Humans remain accountable** for AI-assisted decisions

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

*Use AI as a powerful assistant — not a trusted authority.*

</v-click>
